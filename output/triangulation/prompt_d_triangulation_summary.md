## Acuvue

- The scraped data confirms the reference research framework in identifying key purchase barriers associated with Acuvue. Specifically, concerns about price (114 mentions) and previous negative experiences (72 mentions) align with expectations that cost and past interactions significantly influence consumer decisions. The mention of bad reviews (70) also resonates with the anticipated skepticism toward unfamiliar products.

- However, a divergence is evident regarding consumer sentiments tied to the attributes of Acuvue. The framework suggests that packaging and delivery logistics are not significant barriers to purchase, yet scraped data reveals high mention totals for fast delivery (108), packaging issues (67), and fast shipping (55). This may indicate that logistical factors play a larger role in consumer perception than initially accounted for — warranting further exploration into whether shipping efficiency and packaging contribute to overall brand loyalty.

- The mentions pertaining to brand loyalty and familiarity (51 mentions of "used to current brand") highlight an experience separate from the reference framework. This suggests a potentially underestimated dynamic where consumers exhibit resistance to switching brands purely based on previous usage patterns, which could be a deeper insight into habitual buying behavior.

- On the attribute-importance quadrant, while the totals for Basic Requirements (312) and Key Drivers (326) are consistent with expected consumer priorities, the relatively low mentions (63) in the Low Importance category suggest a need for deeper investigation. It raises the hypothesis that consumers might perceive a narrower range of attributes as crucial compared to what the reference framework anticipates.

- Finally, the absence of barriers related to fashion/trends and visibility in advertising (both zero-match across brands) diverges from the expectation that aesthetic appeal and promotional visibility significantly influence purchase behavior. This gap could suggest that Acuvue's target demographic is less driven by fashion or promotional strategies, indicating an opportunity to rethink marketing strategies and messaging approaches.

## Bausch & Lomb

The examination of external scraped review and social data for Bausch & Lomb aligns with the reference research framework in several areas, notably in the identification of purchase barriers. The most frequently mentioned barriers—primarily the perceptions of high price (105 mentions) and previous negative experiences (73 mentions)—confirm the framework's emphasis on cost and satisfaction as significant deterrents to purchase. Additionally, concerns about brand loyalty (60 mentions), largely stemming from familiarity with competitor brands, reflect established insights into customer retention and brand-switching dynamics.

However, the scraped data diverges from the reference framework in its identification of additional recurring themes in customer feedback. Specifically, while the framework outlines traditional purchase barriers, it overlooks the substantial mention of delivery-related concerns, such as fast delivery (108 mentions) and shipping efficiency (55 mentions). This suggests that convenience plays a more critical role in the purchasing decision for Bausch & Lomb than the reference framework accounts for—worth flagging as a real signal or a data gap.

Another noteworthy divergence is the emphasis on packaging issues (67 mentions), which, while not categorized as a barrier in the reference framework, reveals an underlying consumer concern that could affect brand perception. Hypothesis: If packaging quality significantly influences customer satisfaction, it may warrant further investigation to understand its potential impact on purchase behavior.

Furthermore, the distribution of mentions across the attribute-importance quadrants indicates a robust perception of essential product features, with Basic Requirements (255) and Key Drivers (276) both receiving considerable attention. However, the low importance associated with only 45 mentions in the Low Importance category suggests that many attributes that might typically fall into lesser significance are, in fact, perceived as vital. Hypothesis: The emphasis on these attributes could imply that more elements of the product experience contribute to customer loyalty than the reference framework captures. 

In summary, while Bausch & Lomb's external reviews largely confirm the research framework’s insights on key purchase barriers, the indication of convenience-related issues and packaging concerns highlights areas for further exploration, challenging some of the framework's assumptions regarding customer priorities.
